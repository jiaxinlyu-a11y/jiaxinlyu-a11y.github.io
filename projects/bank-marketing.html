Case study · Logistic regression · R and SAS

Which bank clients say yes to a term deposit?

I ran the same logistic regression twice, once in R and once in SAS, and checked every result against the other. The question: which clients agree to a term deposit, and how well can that be predicted before the call is made?

01The data

The public UCI Bank Marketing data: phone campaigns of a Portuguese bank (Moro, Laureano and Cortez, 2011). Each row is one client who was contacted; the outcome is whether they subscribed to a term deposit.

45,211clients contacted by phone, May 2008 to Nov 2010
11.7%subscribed (5,289 clients), so most calls end in “no”
17variables: job, age, loans, previous campaign, call length and more

03What I found

Job matters. Students (28.7%) and retired clients (22.8%) subscribe most, blue-collar workers (7.3%) least.

A successful previous campaign stands out. With all variables in one model, it has by far the largest odds ratio (9.5). Housing loans, personal loans, being blue-collar and extra contacts all go with lower odds.

One variable would make the model look much better than it is. Adding the length of the call lifts the AUC from 0.74 to 0.89 on held-out data. But the call length is known only after the call, so this is target leakage and the honest model leaves it out.

Bottom line. Using only what is known before the call, the model separates subscribers from non-subscribers modestly (test AUC 0.74) and beats a no-information guess (Brier score 0.089 against 0.104). These are associations in observational data, not causes.
