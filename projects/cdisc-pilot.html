Case study · Clinical trial programming · SAS and R

From raw trial data to report tables, the CDISC way

I took the public CDISC pilot study from its standard SDTM tables to the two tables every trial report starts with, and checked each dataset with an independent R program. This is the daily work of a statistical programmer: SDTM → ADaM → TLF.

01The data

CDISCPILOT01, a public example study published by CDISC and PHUSE: a 26-week trial of xanomeline skin patches in Alzheimer’s disease, with placebo, a low dose (54 mg) and a high dose (81 mg). The data come as SDTM tables in SAS transport files (.xpt), the format regulators require.

254subjects randomised (306 screened, 52 screen failures removed)
4SDTM tables used: DM, DS, EX and AE
1,191adverse events, 1,126 of them after the first dose

02The pipeline

Each layer has one job. SDTM records what happened. ADaM works out everything the analysis needs, once, so every table uses the same groups and flags. TLF only counts and lays out.

Key terms.

TermStands forIn one line
SDTMStudy Data Tabulation ModelThe collected data in a standard layout: what happened, recorded as is.
ADaMAnalysis Data ModelSDTM reshaped for analysis: new variables, flags and real dates worked out once.
TLFTables, Listings and FiguresThe outputs that go into the clinical study report.
ADSLADaM Subject-Level datasetOne row per subject. Every trial has one; every table takes its groups and flags from it.
ADAEADaM Adverse Event datasetOne row per adverse event, with the subject’s treatment and analysis dates added.
ITTIntent-to-treat populationEveryone randomised, analysed by the arm they were assigned to. Used for efficacy and baseline tables.
SafetySafety populationEveryone who took at least one dose, analysed by the arm they actually received.
TEAETreatment-emergent adverse eventAn adverse event that started on or after the first dose.

03Building ADSL

One row per subject. It starts from DM, drops screen failures, then adds treatment groups, real dates, population flags and the end-of-study status from DS.

StepWhat it doesVariables addedCheck
2.1Read DM from the transport file—306 subjects
2.2Drop the 52 screen failures (never randomised, never dosed)—254 left: 86 / 84 / 84
2.3Planned and actual arm, each with a number for sorting (0 / 54 / 81 mg, the doses in EX)TRT01P(N), TRT01A(N)12 subjects planned 81 mg received 54 mg
2.4Turn text dates into SAS dates; days on treatment; who took a doseTRTSDT, TRTEDT, TRTDURD, SAFFLSAFFL = Y for all 254; 2 have no last-dose date
2.5Age group and ITT flagAGEGR1(N), ITTFL<65: 33, 65–80: 144, >80: 77
2.6Keep, order and label 22 columns; save as .sas7bdat and .xpt—254 × 22
2.7Merge DS (one disposition record per subject): completed or why they leftEOSSTT, DCSREASCompleted 58 / 25 / 27

Planned versus actual arm. Twelve subjects assigned to 81 mg actually received 54 mg. Efficacy and baseline tables keep the randomised groups (86 / 84 / 84); safety tables count what entered the body (86 / 96 / 72). That is why ADSL carries both TRT01P and TRT01A.

04Building ADAE

One row per adverse event. It joins AE to ADSL by USUBJID, keeping every event, so each event knows its subject’s actual treatment and first-dose date.

StepWhat it doesVariables addedCheck
4.1Read AE—1,191 events from 225 subjects
4.2Merge the actual arm and treatment dates from ADSL by USUBJID; keep every AE rowTRTA(N), TRTSDT, TRTEDT, SAFFLEvents: 301 / 454 / 436
4.3Analysis dates; fill in partial start dates and flag what was filledASTDT, ASTDTF, AENDTASTDT never missing
4.4Flag events that started on or after the first dose; keep, order, label and saveTRTEMFLTEAE: 1,126; before dosing: 65

Partial dates. Some start dates were recorded without a day or a month. Without a full date an event cannot be compared with the first dose, so it is filled in by a fixed rule and flagged. End dates were either complete or blank; a blank end date means the event was still ongoing.

AESTDTC looks likeCountRuleASTDTF
2013-05-14 (full)1,165use as isblank
2013-05 (no day)15day → 01D

Treatment-emergent. TRTEMFL = "Y" when the start date is on or after the first dose. The 65 events that began before dosing cannot be blamed on the drug and stay out of the safety table.

06Same answer in R and SAS

Double programming is the standard quality check in the industry: a second program, written separately, must reproduce every value. R read the same SDTM files, rebuilt each dataset without looking at the SAS output, then compared the two value by value after sorting both the same way.

What was comparedValues comparedMismatchesMatch
ADSL, built again in R from DM254 rows × 22 columns0
ADAE, built again in R from AE and ADSL1,191 rows × 20 columns0
Table 14.2.1 and Table 14.3.1every number in both tables0

Bottom line. The datasets and the two report tables are traceable from SDTM to the final numbers, and two independent programs agree on every one of them. Most of the work is in ADaM; once ADSL and ADAE are right, the tables are mainly counting and layout.
