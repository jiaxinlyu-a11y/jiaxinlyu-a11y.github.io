Building a standard EHS product, then tailoring it to each customer

An EHS management system. I benchmarked the market, compared three existing systems, and turned what I found into design decisions for a standard product, then adjusted that design to a customer’s own processes.

01Why a standard product

The existing system fell short in three ways, and the plan had three phases.

02Benchmarking the market

I reviewed five vendors and built a list of about 196 core functions, then marked where our system matched, needed work or had nothing.

03Three systems compared

I then compared three working systems function by function: A, a system delivered to a customer; B, a simpler system built to a customer’s brief; and C, a market product.

04Design decisions

The comparison left a set of design questions about how plans, training and checks should behave. These three shaped the standard product.

05Tailoring to a customer

The same design was then applied to one customer’s own processes. Access is configured by role, and four things were adjusted.

06Conclusion

The benchmark showed what existing systems get wrong: scattered entries, repeated pages and inconsistent fields. The standard product avoids them, and the customer adjustments follow the same rules.
