Choosing a model I could defend: re-analysing a retracted imaging study

MSc thesis. A published study claimed that plaque predicts plaque and ApoB does not. I re-analysed its released data to test that claim, and every step below is a problem I hit, the options I weighed, and the evidence I used to choose.

01Why re-analyse

The article was retracted in 2026, and the released data did not reproduce its published figures. Four concerns shaped the re-analysis.

02Question and strategy

Do the available data support the original interpretation when the published results are reproduced, diagnosed and extended? I turned each concern into a method.

03Overlapping signals

Every baseline measure predicts later plaque strongly on its own. But they largely measure the same thing, so these slopes show association, not independent contribution.

04Main models on the raw scale

I compared three full models that code CAC differently, started from the one with the lowest AIC, and removed one term at a time using p-values and AIC.

05Checks: log scale and change score

Because the data are skewed and the outcome can be defined in more than one way, I repeated the modelling on a log scale and on the change in NCPV. These are sensitivity checks on the raw-scale models, not replacements.

06Diagnostics

The final raw model fits very well (adjusted R² 0.961), but a strong fit does not make the inference valid. I checked its assumptions and what each remedy changed.

07Rebuilding ApoB

Participant-level ApoB was not in the released data, only in a published scatterplot. I built a small tool to read the values back, and made it measure its own accuracy.

With the rebuilt values I ran the ApoB models the original claim implies: baseline ApoB and change in ApoB against follow-up and change in NCPV, then ApoB added to the baseline-NCPV model.

08Conclusion

The re-analysis supports a qualified version of the original conclusion, not a simple confirmation or rejection. The data back the broad finding that existing plaque predicts later plaque, but not every part of the title to the same degree.
