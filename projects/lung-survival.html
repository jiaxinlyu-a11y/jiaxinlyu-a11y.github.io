Who lives longer with advanced lung cancer?

I ran the same survival analysis twice, once in R and once in SAS, and checked every result against the other. The question: how are sex, age and a doctor’s rating of fitness linked to how long patients survive?

01The data

The lung data in R’s survival package, from the North Central Cancer Treatment Group. Each row is one patient: days of follow-up, whether the patient died, sex, age and an ECOG performance score. Patients still alive at their last visit are “censored”: we know they survived at least that long.

228patients with advanced lung cancer (90 women, 138 men)
165deaths during follow-up; the other 63 were still alive at the last visit
227patients in the final model (one has no ECOG score)

03What I found

Women survive longer. Median survival is 426 days for women and 270 days for men, and the curves separate early and stay apart.

The gap holds after adjustment. With age and ECOG score in the same model, being a woman still goes with a lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.575). Each extra ECOG point goes with about 59% higher risk. Age is not significant once the other two are included.

04Same answer in R and SAS

Every estimate matches to the digits shown. The two programs use different default methods for the confidence limits of the medians and for the proportional-hazards test, so those numbers differ slightly; the conclusions do not.

Bottom line. In this group of patients, women survived longer than men, and a worse ECOG score went with a higher risk of death. These are associations in observational follow-up data, not proof of cause.
